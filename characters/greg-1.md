# Greg #1 — The Regular Dad

## Role
Deadpan, reluctant testimonial guy. He's not selling you anything — that's the joke and the whole persuasion mechanism. He tried the thing because someone made him, it worked, and he's mildly annoyed about it.

## Demographic target
Dads late-40s to early-50s see themselves in him. Their kids find him funny and send him to the group chat — that's the distribution hack. Dual-audience by design.

## Appearance (locked — use verbatim in prompts)
Man in his early 50s, average build with a slight dad gut, short graying hair, weekend stubble. Wears a plain polo or flannel, jeans, white sneakers. Default set: front porch or garage, natural daylight. Never styled, never glamorous, never gym-fit.

## Voice (locked)
- Deadpan Midwestern. Short sentences. Long pauses do the work.
- Dad-joke humor, delivered flat — he does not laugh at his own jokes.
- Self-deprecating, wife-referencing: "My wife told me not to do this." "I did it anyway. That part's on me."
- Reluctant, never enthusiastic. The strongest endorsement Greg gives is "…it worked. I don't love that it worked."
- Understates everything. If the result was dramatic, Greg calls it "fine, I guess."

## Forbidden
- Exclamation points. Hype words ("amazing", "insane", "game-changer").
- Explaining the joke. Winking at the camera. Enthusiasm of any kind.
- Medical claims — Greg reports what happened to Greg, nothing more.

## Formats
Primary: Greg 1.0 porch testimonial (see `greg-testimonial-format` skill — 4-clip structure is locked there). Secondary: dad-joke remedy videos in `formats/debunk-flip-30.md` where the debunk is something his kid/wife showed him.

## Funnel
MD Klean castor oil and the detox guide. Greg is proof-of-ordinary-person; he never explains mechanisms (that's Dr. Becky's job) — he explains that he's a normal guy and it still worked.
