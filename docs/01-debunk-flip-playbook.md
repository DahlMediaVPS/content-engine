# The Debunk-Flip Playbook

The repeatable structure behind every debunk video. This is the strategy layer; the beat-by-beat script structure lives in the `jasons-copywriting` skill (6 beats, locked).

## Anatomy of a debunk-flip

### 1. Hook — the visual debunk (0–3s)
Show the claim being tested, not talked about. Hot water poured over an apple, waxy film lifting off. Cinnamon sprinkled on a banana. The viewer has *seen this hack before* — that's the point. Recognition is the hook.

Rule: the hook must be a claim the viewer has already encountered in their feed. We are not introducing ideas; we are settling arguments.

### 2. The verdict (3–10s)
Fast, confident, specific: "Guaranteed doesn't work." Give the one-sentence reason why. No hedging, no "studies suggest." The debunk beat is where we spend zero credibility and earn all of it.

Viewer's internal line we're writing toward: *"Yeah. That's what I thought."*

### 3. The flip (10–25s)
Never leave them empty-handed — that's a takedown channel, not a funnel. Same ingredient or same desire, redirected:

- "Cinnamon on a banana? Nothing. Cinnamon in [real use]? That actually does something — here's what."
- "That waxy film IS real though — here's what it is and what to actually do about it."

The flip is where the intentional-dialogue rules apply hardest (see `engine/consistency-rules.md`): the remedy must have real steps, a real mechanism sentence, and a real expected outcome. This is what separates us from the people we debunk.

### 4. The bridge (25s–end)
One CTA, one funnel. Free guide first — the guide carries the castor oil detox and the members-group invite, so the video only has to sell the *click*, not the product.

Bridge lines acknowledge skepticism instead of fighting it: "You've probably heard of it. You're probably just using it wrong." That line only works because beats 1–2 proved we're willing to say things *don't* work.

## Enemy rules

- **Attack claims, never named creators.** "That hack going around" / "these creators telling you" — never a handle, never a face. Named attacks invite drama, takedowns, and defamation risk; unnamed enemies let every viewer supply their own.
- **Include the audience in the win.** "You knew it was fake" — the viewer debunked it *with* us. Stolen audiences convert; humiliated audiences don't.
- **Never punch at the desire.** The viewer's wish (clean food, young skin, self-reliance) is always valid; only the method was wrong. We debunk the method, then serve the desire.

## Debunk : flip content ratio

Run roughly **2 debunks : 1 pure flip** per character per day. All-debunk feeds burn out (negativity fatigue) and never build the "this actually works" library the funnel needs. The pure-flip videos reference the debunk history: "I've told you what doesn't work. This works."

## Hook sourcing

The scraper (`engine/scraper-spec.md`) feeds `hooks/backlog.md`. A good debunk target scores on:

1. **Saturation** — how many times has the viewer seen this claim? (Higher = better hook)
2. **Debunkability** — can we show it failing visually in under 5 seconds?
3. **Flip distance** — how close is the real remedy to one of our three funnels? (Closer = better)

A viral claim we can't flip toward a funnel is a maybe. A moderately viral claim that flips straight into castor oil or skin routine is a yes.
