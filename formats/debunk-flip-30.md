# Format: Debunk-Flip 30

**Length:** 30 seconds · **Primary character:** Greg #2 · **Structure:** 3 clips

The volume workhorse. One claim in, one verdict, one flip, one CTA. If the debunk needs more than one demonstration or the flip needs more than three steps, it's a Debunk-Flip 60 — don't cram.

## Clip structure

### Clip 1 — Visual debunk hook (0–5s)
The claim being tested on camera, failing (or revealing — the apple-wax reveal is a hook even though the film is real; the debunk is what the *other creators say it means*). No intro, no "hey guys." First frame is the demonstration already in motion.

- Dialogue starts at ~1s, over the action: name the claim the viewer already knows. "Cinnamon on a banana. You've seen it."
- **Intentional action rule applies:** the script must specify the physical action and its visible result, not "shows the hack." (See `engine/consistency-rules.md`.)

### Clip 2 — Verdict + flip (5–22s)
- Verdict in one breath: "Guaranteed doesn't work. Here's why —" + one-sentence mechanism.
- Hard turn to the flip: same ingredient/desire, real use. Steps numbered, mechanism in one sentence, expected outcome stated ("what you'll actually notice, and when").
- The flip gets the majority of screen time. We're a remedy channel that debunks, not a debunk channel.

### Clip 3 — Bridge CTA (22–30s)
One funnel, one action. Default: free guide. Skeptic-validating close: "Everything that actually works, one free guide. You've earned the skepticism — that's why it's free."

Comment-gate variant (per `jasons-copywriting` gate beat): "Comment APPLE and I'll send it to you."

## Render notes
- Direct-to-camera, close framing, practical set per character file.
- Realism blocks from `davemedia` skill mandatory (hard-flash for skin, macro raking light for food demos).
- Text overlay: claim name in clip 1, step numbers in clip 2, CTA word in clip 3. Nothing else.

## QA additions for this format
- Would a viewer who has seen the original hack recognize it within 2 seconds? If not, the hook fails.
- Does the flip survive the "so what do I actually do" test — could the viewer do it tonight from the script alone?
